# Targeted primary-source verification pass

Date: 2026-08-29

Scope: four high-risk control topics selected from the archive. This is not a corpus-wide systematic review. “Verified” here means the bibliographic record and abstract-level finding were checked against PubMed; it does not mean the finding has been converted into an executable rule or validated for THE Hybrid System.

## Verified source facts

### Concurrent training

An updated systematic review/meta-analysis included 43 studies of healthy adults comparing supervised concurrent aerobic-plus-strength training with the same strength prescription alone. Its abstract reports no clear group-level decrement for maximal strength (SMD -0.06, 95% CI -0.20 to 0.09) or hypertrophy (SMD -0.01, 95% CI -0.16 to 0.18), but a decrement for explosive strength (SMD -0.28, 95% CI -0.48 to -0.08). This supports context-specific interference handling, not a blanket cardio penalty. Source: [Schumann et al., 2022, PMID 34757594](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/).

### Acute:chronic workload ratio

The cited conceptual critique states that causal manipulation of ACWR to reduce injury remains unsupported and that ratio properties can add ambiguity, noise, and artifacts. This supports retaining contextual load history while rejecting a universal ACWR injury-danger threshold. Source: [Impellizzeri et al., 2020, PMID 32502973](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32502973/).

### Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport

The 2023 IOC consensus describes RED-S as a complex syndrome associated with low energy availability, individual moderating factors, accumulated severity and risk stratification, and introduces a clinical assessment tool. The source is a consensus statement, not a validation of a single app threshold or an automated diagnosis. THE Hybrid System therefore requires a non-diagnostic review/hold pathway and qualified clinical ownership before any related rule can be activated. Source: [Mountjoy et al., 2023, PMID 37752011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37752011/).

### HRV-guided training

A methodological systematic review/meta-analysis found HRV-guided training superior for vagal-related HRV indices (SMD 0.50, 95% CI 0.09 to 0.91), while fitness/performance effects were small and statistically non-significant in the reported pooled results. The authors also identify unresolved methodological choices for index selection, recording position, baselines and daily-change interpretation. This supports HRV as a protocol-specific advisory input with uncertainty, not a universal readiness gate. Source: [Manresa-Rocamora et al., 2021, PMID 34639599](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34639599/).
