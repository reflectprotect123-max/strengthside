# THE HYBRID ENGINE
## Standard Operating Procedures

**Operating principle:** Built, not burnt.

These SOPs define how the Hybrid system is selected, delivered, progressed, adjusted, and reviewed. They are written for the current four-week block model: two full-body strength sessions and two separate conditioning sessions.

The system is designed for the worst week, not the perfect week.

---

## SOP-001 — System Chassis

### Purpose

Keep the weekly system simple, repeatable, and resilient when work, sleep, equipment, or life changes.

### Default weekly layout

| Day | Session | Intent |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Strength A | Full-body strength; squat-dominant emphasis |
| Tuesday | Easy modality conditioning | Build capacity without creating excessive fatigue |
| Wednesday | Flexible recovery day | Walking, mobility, recovery, or life demands |
| Thursday | Strength B | Full-body strength; hinge/pull/press emphasis |
| Friday | Moderate mixed conditioning | Controlled pacing and work capacity |
| Saturday | Flexible recovery day | Low-intensity movement or rest |
| Sunday | Flexible recovery day | Rest and preparation |

### Non-negotiables

- Every complete week contains two strength sessions and two conditioning sessions.
- Strength and conditioning remain separate by default.
- The two strength sessions are not scheduled back-to-back.
- Conditioning is modality-first: Run, Bike, Rower, Ski Erg, or Sled.
- No generic circuit entry replaces a defined conditioning purpose.
- The system progresses inside four-week blocks.
- The plan must work when the athlete has a bad week.

### Minimum viable week

| Level | Completed work |
|---|---|
| Floor | Two strength sessions |
| Base | Two strength sessions plus one conditioning session |
| Full chassis | Two strength sessions plus two conditioning sessions |

The athlete never “makes up” a missed session by stacking excessive work into the next day.

## SOP-002 — Block Setup and Priority

### Purpose

Decide what the current block is trying to improve without creating competing programs.

### Priority hierarchy

Each block identifies:

1. **Primary quality:** the main limiter or highest-return target.
2. **Secondary quality:** developed or maintained with a controlled dose.
3. **Maintenance qualities:** kept present so nothing important disappears.

The default Hybrid emphasis is muscle and strength first, conditioning and recovery second, and power third. This can change when the athlete’s actual limiter changes.

### Block-start questions

- What is the one quality that would improve the whole system if it improved?
- What must be maintained while that quality is prioritised?
- What training can the athlete realistically complete for four weeks?
- What equipment and modalities are actually available?
- What would make this block fail: excessive fatigue, poor schedule fit, pain, or lack of progression?

### Block rules

- Keep the weekly chassis stable.
- Keep the main lifts fixed unless there is a clear equipment, pain, or programming reason to change them.
- Change dose, intensity, exercise emphasis, or conditioning progression—not the entire system.
- Do not add a second program on top of the Hybrid plan.

## SOP-003 — Readiness Check

### Purpose

Choose the correct version of today’s session without turning readiness into a complicated scoring system.

### The three-minute check

Before training, record:

- Sleep and general energy
- Current soreness or pain
- Work and life stress
- Expected training intent for today

### Decision rules

| Status | Decision |
|---|---|
| Ready | Complete the planned session and progress if earned |
| Managed | Keep the main intent; reduce optional work, density, or conditioning duration |
| Not ready | Use the minimum effective version or recover; do not force a test |
| Pain or acute injury concern | Stop loading the affected area and seek qualified clinical guidance |

Readiness changes the dose before it changes the purpose. A tired moderate conditioning day becomes shorter and controlled; it does not become a random circuit. A short strength session keeps the main lift and removes the finisher first.

## SOP-004 — Strength Session Execution

### Purpose

Deliver two efficient full-body sessions that build useful strength without burying the athlete.

### Session order

1. **General warm-up** — raise temperature and move through the relevant ranges.
2. **Plyometric primer** — prescribed 3 × 3 fast contacts where appropriate; stop before fatigue.
3. **Movement primer** — prepare the positions and pattern for the main work.
4. **Main mover one** — the priority lift.
5. **Main mover two** — the secondary compound pattern.
6. **Accessories** — targeted support work, normally leaving 2–3 reps in reserve.
7. **Recovery breathing** — downshift before leaving.

### Main lift rules

- Use the prescribed set and rep scheme for the block.
- Build to a heavy set of the day without turning the session into a max test.
- Beat last week’s load when the prescribed reps are achieved with good execution.
- If the load cannot increase, beat last week’s reps within the prescribed range.
- Use the smallest available jump.
- Keep the main lift around the planned effort ceiling; do not grind for ego.
- If the athlete is managed, protect the main lift and remove optional volume first.

### Accessory rules

- Accessories support the main patterns; they do not compete with them.
- Leave 2–3 reps in reserve unless a specific block says otherwise.
- Progress load or reps only when the target quality is maintained.
- If time is limited, remove accessories before main movers.
- Avoid unnecessary exercise rotation during the block.

### Time rule

Target approximately 45 minutes. Sixty minutes is the ceiling unless there is a specific reason to exceed it.

## SOP-005 — Strength Progression and Logger

### Purpose

Make progression obvious and remove guesswork from the next session.

### Logger display

Every main lift must display:

- Current prescription
- Working Max
- Last
- Actual result
- Next-session target

**Working Max:** use the agreed working-max convention, such as 90% of the athlete’s best-ever performance.

**Last:** display the previous week’s set-by-set numbers beside the current prescription. Example:

```text
Prescription: 4 sets — 8, 8, 6, 6
Working Max: 90% of best ever
Last: 82.5 kg — 8, 8, 6, 6
Today: smallest available jump if earned
```

### Progression decision

1. Did the athlete complete the prescribed reps?
2. Was technique acceptable?
3. Was the effort within the planned ceiling?
4. Can the smallest load jump be made?
5. If not, can reps improve within the range?

The next target is either:

- Heavier at the same quality;
- More reps at the same load; or
- The same load and reps when the athlete needs to consolidate.

No automatic increase is awarded for ugly reps, pain, or a maximal grind.

## SOP-006 — Conditioning Modality Selection

### Purpose

Select one conditioning tool and develop it long enough for progression to mean something.

### Available modalities

- Run
- Bike
- Rower
- Ski Erg
- Sled

### Selection rules

- Choose the modality that matches the current goal, equipment, joints, and schedule.
- Keep the modality stable for the block unless it causes pain or cannot be performed.
- Substitute within the same demand when equipment changes.
- Do not change modality simply because the session feels ordinary.
- Do not use circuits to hide an undefined conditioning goal.

### Conditioning intensity language

| Intent | Working effort | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Easy | RPE 4–6 | Conversational and sustainable |
| Moderate | RPE 6–7 | Controlled urgency; demanding but repeatable |
| Hard | RPE 8–9 | Used selectively, not as the default |
| Test | RPE 10 | Assessment only |

Most Hybrid conditioning is capped at RPE 7 or below. The Tuesday session must help Thursday. Friday must not bury the next week’s strength work.

## SOP-007 — Easy Modality Conditioning

### Purpose

Build the aerobic base and recoverability without stealing from strength progression.

### Execution

- Use one selected modality.
- Stay conversational and controlled.
- Use the block’s prescribed work and recovery intervals.
- Progress duration, interval length, or total controlled output—not random intensity.
- Walking or a reduction in pace is permitted if needed to preserve easy intent.
- Do not chase watch pace or turn the session into a time trial.

### Pass standard

The athlete finishes feeling trained, not damaged. Thursday strength should be better supported by Tuesday, not compromised by it.

---

## SOP-008 — Moderate Mixed Conditioning

### Purpose

Develop pacing, controlled urgency, and repeatable work capacity while keeping the selected modality central.

### Execution

- Start with the selected modality.
- Use the block’s defined intervals, rest, and supporting movement pattern.
- Keep the modality as the anchor; supporting work must not turn the session into a random circuit.
- Cap normal training at the prescribed moderate effort, generally RPE 6–7.
- Finish with enough control that the athlete can recover for the next week.
- Hard efforts and testing require an explicit block purpose; they are never added casually.

### Pass standard

The athlete completes the work at controlled urgency, with consistent pacing and no collapse in the final interval.

## SOP-009 — Missed Sessions, Schedule Disruption, and External PT

### Purpose

Keep the system intact when the week is not perfect.

### If a session is missed

- Move it to the next suitable day when practical.
- Do not place the two strength sessions back-to-back.
- Do not double the missed volume.
- If the week is compressed, protect both strength sessions first.
- Use the base week: two strength sessions plus one conditioning session.
- Use the floor when necessary: two strength sessions.

### If mandatory PT or outside training occurs

Record it before the next session. Classify it by:

- Modality or movement demand
- Intensity
- Duration
- Impact and fatigue

It may count as a conditioning exposure only when its actual demand matches the intended conditioning session. Otherwise, it is an external stressor that informs the next dose.

### Equipment change

Substitute the movement pattern or conditioning demand, not the entire session. Keep the intent and progression intact.

## SOP-010 — Recovery and Downshift

### Purpose

Start recovery before the athlete leaves the session.

### End-of-session routine

- Complete the prescribed recovery breathing.
- Record the essential training result.
- Note unusual pain, fatigue, or performance changes.
- Leave the gym; do not add “extra credit” because the session felt short.

### Weekly recovery floor

- Protect sleep as the foundation.
- Maintain normal low-intensity movement where practical.
- Fuel the hard training days.
- Do not use soreness as proof that the session worked.
- Treat recovery as part of training, not as time lost from training.

## SOP-011 — Four-Week Block Review

### Purpose

Decide what to carry forward before changing the program.

### Review data

- Main-lift load and reps compared with Last
- Accessory performance at 2–3 RIR
- Easy conditioning output at controlled effort
- Moderate conditioning pacing and completion
- Session compliance
- Sleep, stress, and recovery pattern
- Whether Tuesday helped Thursday
- Whether Friday buried the following week

### Review questions

1. What improved?
2. What was maintained?
3. What became the limiting factor?
4. Did the athlete recover well enough to repeat quality work?
5. Was the program too easy, too hard, or appropriately demanding?
6. What is the single highest-return priority for the next block?

### Change rule

Change the smallest thing that solves the problem. Keep successful structure, exercises, and modalities stable.

---

## SOP-012 — Resources and Coaching Notes

### Purpose

Turn education into useful action without creating another training program.

Each resource should contain:

- One clear topic
- A short original summary
- Practical takeaways
- Tags
- Original source link
- Optional timestamp
- One “apply this to training” note

Resources educate the athlete; they do not silently alter the program. Any program change must be made through the current block, priority, and progression rules.

---

## Final operating rule

The Hybrid system should answer four questions every day:

1. What is today’s training purpose?
2. What is the minimum work that moves that purpose forward?
3. What did I do last time?
4. What is the smallest progression or adjustment that lets me repeat quality work?

If the answer requires a complicated explanation, the system has drifted away from KISS.
